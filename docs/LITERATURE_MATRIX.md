# Current literature, claim boundaries, and novelty gate

**Audit date:** 2026-08-23; targeted current-date refresh 2026-08-24<br>
**Scope:** primary papers, official proceedings/publisher pages, official repositories, and versioned dataset records available by the audit date  
**Project stage:** post-confirmatory; the single locked target opening was consumed on 2026-08-24<br>
**Gate result:** benchmark **`pass_with_narrowed_contribution`**; model **`closed_not_supported` / `exploratory_negative`**

## Decision in one page

The strict literature collision was not found in the searched record. No verified work was found that combines all of the following:

1. InclusiveHAR version 4;
2. participant-exclusive source/target separation under an explicitly audited released-block protocol;
3. training and model selection using only non-disabled source participants;
4. a prospectively locked, one-way evaluation on participants with disabilities;
5. strong classical, deep, and domain-generalization baselines under equal information and tuning budgets; and
6. participant-level lower-tail, uncertainty, calibration, and efficiency evidence.

This is a **scoped search result, not proof of novelty**. It is sufficient to continue protocol construction, but it is not sufficient to write “first” in a paper.

The original broad framing is not defensible. Prior work already includes smartphone/wearable recognition involving people with stroke, lower-limb amputations, multiple sclerosis, and mobility aids; healthy/non-impaired-to-disabled transfer tests; disability/fairness analyses; user-independent mobility-aid recognition; broad OOD and SSL benchmarks; sensor-fault evaluation; and activity/style disentanglement. InclusiveHAR itself reports an author-described non-disabled-only training scenario. Therefore, the candidate contribution is narrowed to:

> **An auditable participant-exclusive, released-block, one-way
> ability-associated population-shift benchmark on InclusiveHAR v4, with
> source-only model selection, an explicit activity-realization ontology,
> strong matched baselines, participant-level lower-tail and calibration
> evidence, and a one-time target evaluation.**

The v4 release does not expose timestamps or trial identifiers, and the audit
could not recover trial boundaries. The candidate distinction therefore does
not include “trial-safe,” “trial-exclusive,” “hidden-join-free,” or an
unqualified “leakage-safe” claim. This data limitation is part of the
benchmark result, not a condition assumed away.

This wording describes a **candidate distinction**, not established novelty. The benchmark remains the primary contribution. The completed locked comparison found compact DANN at mean target-participant macro-F1 0.6808438 and full MoRe-HAR at 0.6353323; MoRe-HAR failed both the preregistered mean and joint lower-tail improvement conditions. Its model gate is therefore closed as **not supported**, and the implementation is retained as an exploratory negative result. GILE, AFFAR, BPD, and CMD-HAR already cover activity/person or invariant/specific representation separation, while ContrastSense, SICL, MultiSupConHAR, and CCIL cover closely related subject- or category-invariant learning. No positive model-novelty claim is supported.

## Evidence notation and rules

- **V — verified:** stated in an inspected primary paper, official repository, official dataset record, or publisher/proceedings page.
- **I — inference:** a bounded interpretation derived from verified facts; it is explicitly labelled.
- **N — scoped negative search:** not found in the named sources with the documented queries. It does not prove absence.
- **NR — not reported/verified:** the inspected primary source did not provide enough information for the field.

Metrics below are those reported by the cited work, not independently reproduced here. Preprints are identified as preprints. Repository availability does not imply that its licence permits reuse; an explicit licence must be verified before copying code.

## Critical interpretation of InclusiveHAR’s own baseline

The InclusiveHAR paper must be cited as prior evaluation of the broad idea. The following interpretation is deliberately conservative:

- **V:** the paper defines one scenario using only data from non-disabled individuals for training and contrasts it with mixed training.
- **V:** the authors interpret the first scenario’s very low performance as difficulty recognizing activities performed by participants with disabilities.
- **V:** reported scenario-one accuracy/F1 are 12.87%/12.15%, versus 42.54%/39.37% for mixed training.
- **NR:** the exact test-set cohort, participant identities, trial boundaries, windowing, normalization fit, validation partition, and checkpoint/model-selection procedure are not documented sufficiently to independently verify a participant-exclusive zero-shot experiment.
- **I:** it is fair to call this an **author-described source-only cross-group baseline**. It is not fair to call it a verified participant-exclusive, boundary-audited, locked confirmatory result.

Consequences:

1. Do not claim the first non-disabled-to-disabled InclusiveHAR evaluation.
2. Position the benchmark contribution as making that question auditable,
   participant-exclusive at the source/target level, strongly baselined,
   participant-level, and prospectively locked, while reporting unrecoverable
   within-block trial-boundary risk.
3. Treat the published 12.87%/12.15% result as prior developmental evidence, not as a comparator produced under the new protocol.
4. Preserve the article’s wheelchair label semantics: “walking” is manual propulsion for wheelchair users. Do not rewrite it as ordinary gait.

## Revised contribution and protocol implications

### Candidate benchmark contribution

Use wording no stronger than:

> We construct and audit a participant-exclusive released-block benchmark for
> one-way ability-associated population shift in InclusiveHAR v4. Model
> development and selection use only non-disabled source participants; the
> participant-with-disability cohort is evaluated under a documented lock. The
> benchmark emphasizes participant-level macro-F1, lower-tail performance,
> calibration, uncertainty, reproducible lineage, and explicit accounting for
> unrecoverable within-block trial-boundary risk.

Do not prefix this with “first,” “novel,” or “fair.” Refresh the literature search before submission and let reviewers evaluate distinctness.

### Scientific language

- Report an **ability-associated population shift**, not a causal disability effect.
- Define **zero-shot target transfer** as using no target-participant labels,
  adaptation, normalization, calibration, or model selection. It is not an
  unseen-activity-class claim of the kind studied by ZeroHAR or AnyMo.
- Do not treat people with disabilities as a homogeneous group or infer clinical utility from 10 target participants.
- Do not feed disability or assistive-device status to the inference model in the primary track.
- Keep subgroup/device analyses exploratory because sample sizes are very small.
- Avoid “fairness achieved”; report observed group and participant distributions with uncertainty.

### Ontology consequences from prior work

- UCI-HAR stairs and InclusiveHAR’s 8% ramp are not exact equivalents.
- HAR-PMD’s six classes are mobility modes/aids—ordinary walking, crutches, walker, manual wheelchair, electric wheelchair, still—not alternate labels for InclusiveHAR’s six activities.
- InclusiveHAR wheelchair “walking” means manual propulsion: the shared semantic task may be “mobility,” but the realization must remain explicit.
- Jamieson’s terrain labels and O’Brien/Lonini clinical tasks support the motivation but are not silently pooled with InclusiveHAR.

### MoRe-HAR model gate — closed after the locked evaluation

The target opening is consumed and the preregistered decision is final for this protocol:

1. compact DANN obtained mean target-participant macro-F1 0.6808438, versus 0.6353323 for full MoRe-HAR;
2. full MoRe-HAR did not improve the required mean or joint worst-participant/lower-decile endpoints, although the pre-opening source non-inferiority gate had passed;
3. the full objective did not show monotonic benefit as components were added; the content/consistency ablation was the strongest MoRe-family target result;
4. GILE, AFFAR, BPD, and CMD-HAR make generic content/person or invariant/specific factorization non-novel, while ContrastSense, SICL, MultiSupConHAR, and CCIL make source-subject/category-invariant learning non-novel; and
5. MoRe-HAR must be retained as an exploratory negative result. Target-informed retuning or a post hoc positive model claim is prohibited.

The local post-confirmatory CCIL/BPD adaptations are paper-derived, non-faithful approximations and cannot establish that the official predecessors were fairly outperformed. They remain labelled post-confirmatory and do not reopen the model gate.

combination, or authoritative new InclusiveHAR trial metadata that permits a
stronger trial-exclusive protocol and changes the current released-block risk
assessment.

Remaining risks:

- Direct Google Scholar results were not available to this automated audit; a manual authenticated search is required before submission.
- IEEE/ACM/PMLR full-text indexing and very recent 2026 preprints can lag.
- Several 2026 sources are preprints and can change; pin the audited version and rerun the matrix when a new version appears.
- Repository presence is not a licence. HAROOD, BenchHAR, HARBench, STORM,
  TRI-HAR, Inertia-1, AnyMo, LITEWAY, GILE, ContrastSense, SICL, and CMD-HAR
  require the recorded licence resolution before code reuse; AnyMo Bench also
  carries non-commercial dataset terms inherited from Nymeria. ContrastSense's
  README licence statement is not a substitute for a repository licence file.
- “Zero-shot” is overloaded in HAR. Every manuscript use must say whether the
  held-out variable is the activity class, dataset/setup, participant, or target
  population; this benchmark holds out target participants/population while
  retaining a fixed shared activity ontology.
- Reported metrics in this document are literature claims, not reproduced results.
